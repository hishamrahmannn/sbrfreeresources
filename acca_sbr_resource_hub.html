IAS & IFRS — very short notes

Click a standard to reveal a quick revision point. Use these as memory triggers, not as a replacement for the full syllabus or current ACCA materials.

IAS 1 — PresentationStructure, classification & disclosure
Focus on presentation, current/non-current classification, going concern and material information. Check current IFRS requirements because IFRS 18 changes presentation from 2027.
IAS 2 — InventoriesLower of cost and NRV
Cost includes purchase/conversion and other costs to bring inventory to present condition. Measure at lower of cost and NRV.
IAS 7 — Cash FlowsOperating, investing, financing
Classify cash flows into operating, investing and financing. SBR often tests classification, presentation and interpretation.
IAS 8 — Policies, Estimates & ErrorsRetrospective vs prospective
Policy changes are generally retrospective; estimate changes are prospective. Prior-period errors are corrected retrospectively, subject to practicability.
IAS 10 — Events After ReportingAdjusting vs non-adjusting
Adjusting events provide evidence of conditions existing at reporting date. Non-adjusting events arise after reporting date and may require disclosure if material.
IAS 12 — Income TaxesCurrent & deferred tax
Understand temporary differences, deferred tax assets/liabilities and recognition principles. Link tax effects to the underlying transaction.
IAS 16 — PPERecognition, depreciation, revaluation
Recognise when probable benefits and reliable measurement criteria are met. Depreciate significant components separately. Revaluation has specific OCI/P&L rules.
IAS 19 — Employee BenefitsDefined benefit accounting
For defined benefits, understand service cost, net interest and remeasurement. Remeasurements are recognised in OCI and are not recycled.
IAS 21 — Foreign ExchangeFunctional & presentation currency
Understand initial recognition, subsequent translation, foreign operations and exchange differences. Distinguish transaction effects from translation effects.
IAS 23 — Borrowing CostsQualifying assets
Capitalise directly attributable borrowing costs during the period of activities necessary to prepare a qualifying asset for intended use or sale.
IAS 32 — Financial InstrumentsLiability vs equity
Classification depends on the contractual substance, especially the obligation to deliver cash or another financial asset. Convertible instruments can require split accounting.
IAS 36 — ImpairmentCGU, recoverable amount
Recoverable amount is higher of value in use and fair value less costs of disposal. Allocate goodwill to CGUs/groups of CGUs for impairment testing.
IAS 37 — ProvisionsPresent obligation + probable outflow
Recognise a provision when there is a present obligation from a past event, probable outflow and reliable estimate. Contingent liabilities are generally disclosed, not recognised.
IAS 38 — IntangiblesResearch vs development
Research is expensed. Development costs are capitalised only when all recognition criteria are satisfied. Consider useful life and amortisation.
IAS 40 — Investment PropertyRental/capital appreciation
Property held for rental income or capital appreciation. Under the fair value model, changes in fair value generally go to profit or loss.
IAS 41 — AgricultureBiological assets & produce
Biological assets are generally measured at fair value less costs to sell, with changes recognised in profit or loss, subject to specific exceptions.
IFRS 3 — Business CombinationsAcquisition method & goodwill
Identify acquirer, acquisition date, consideration and identifiable net assets. Goodwill is broadly the residual after recognising identifiable net assets and relevant consideration/NCI.
IFRS 5 — Held for SaleSeparate presentation & measurement
A qualifying disposal group held for sale is generally measured at lower of carrying amount and fair value less costs to sell and is presented separately.
IFRS 9 — Financial InstrumentsClassification, ECL, hedge accounting
Know amortised cost/FVOCI/FVTPL classification, expected credit losses and basic hedge-accounting concepts. SBR often tests application and explanation.
IFRS 10 — ConsolidationControl = power + returns + link
Control exists when the investor has power over the investee, exposure/rights to variable returns and ability to use power to affect those returns.
IFRS 11 — Joint ArrangementsJoint operation vs joint venture
Joint operation: rights to assets and obligations for liabilities. Joint venture: rights to net assets and generally equity accounting under IAS 28.
IFRS 13 — Fair ValueExit price & hierarchy
Fair value is an exit price in an orderly transaction between market participants at the measurement date. Know Levels 1, 2 and 3 inputs.
IFRS 15 — RevenueFive-step model
Identify contract, identify performance obligations, determine transaction price, allocate price, recognise revenue when/as performance obligations are satisfied.
IFRS 16 — LeasesROU asset + lease liability
For lessees, most leases create a right-of-use asset and lease liability, subject to exemptions. Understand initial measurement and subsequent depreciation/interest.
IFRS 18 — PresentationNew presentation/disclosure requirements
Introduces new presentation requirements including defined profit-or-loss subtotals and management-defined performance measures. Check the applicable ACCA exam syllabus and effective date.

Standard-wise SBR quiz

Click an option to reveal the explanation. Questions are written for revision and are not official ACCA questions.

1. Under IAS 36, recoverable amount is:
Answer: B. IAS 36 defines recoverable amount as the higher of value in use and fair value less costs of disposal.
2. Which item is normally recognised in OCI under IAS 19 for a defined benefit plan?
Answer: A. Remeasurements are recognised in OCI and are not subsequently reclassified to profit or loss.
3. Under IFRS 15, revenue is recognised when:
Answer: B. Revenue is recognised when or as the entity satisfies a performance obligation by transferring control of the promised good or service.
4. Which feature is central to identifying control under IFRS 10?
Answer: B. Legal ownership percentage alone does not determine control; the IFRS 10 control model is broader.

Examiner-report: things to learn

General exam-technique reminders. Always read the latest ACCA examiner's reports for the applicable sitting.

Apply, don't just recite

State the relevant accounting requirement, then apply it directly to the facts given in the scenario.

Explain the impact

Where relevant, explain the effect on profit or loss, OCI, assets, liabilities, equity, ratios or disclosures.

Use professional judgement

Identify the issue, discuss the relevant principle, consider the facts and reach a reasoned conclusion.

Don't over-answer

Allocate time to the marks available. Avoid writing long generic textbook paragraphs unrelated to the scenario.

Show calculations clearly

Set out workings and explain the accounting treatment. A correct number without an understandable approach may not fully demonstrate the required skill.

Professional marks

Use clear headings, logical structure, appropriate tone and a report/memo format where the requirement asks for it.
